## Tarot — A.E. Waite & Golden Dawn Sources

### A Manual of Cartomancy and Occult Divination (1909)
Arthur Edward Waite's original published method. Contains the English Method of cartomancy using playing cards, the counting and adjacency rules, and the Significator system. This is the public version of a more complex private method Waite taught within his Order.

- [waite_cartomancy_1909.pdf](waite_cartomancy_1909.pdf)

---

### The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1911)
Waite's companion volume to the Rider-Waite-Smith deck. Contains the Celtic Cross spread, card meanings, and further discussion of the Tarot's symbolism. Published two years after the *Manual*, this is where the Celtic Cross first appeared in print and began its displacement of the older relational method.

- [pictorial_key_tarot_1911.pdf](pictorial_key_tarot_1911.pdf)

---

### Book T: The Tarot (Mathers, 1888)
The internal Golden Dawn manuscript written by MacGregor Mathers. This is the private method Waite was trained in before he simplified it for public consumption. Contains the full five-operation counting system, the Kabalistic attributions, and the Opening of the Key spread. Circulated in manuscript among initiates; never published during Waite's lifetime.

- [mathers_tarot_1888.txt](mathers_tarot_1888.txt)

---

### The Tarot of the Bohemians (Papus, 1892)
Gérard Encausse's codification of the French occult Tarot tradition that preceded and influenced Waite. Contains the counting methods of Etteila and the Egyptian origin theory. Essential context for understanding what Waite inherited and what he changed.

- [papus_tarot_bohemians_1892.txt](papus_tarot_bohemians_1892.txt)

## Geomancy — Western Tradition

### The Geomancie of Maister Christopher Cattan (1591)
The first English-language geomancy text, translated from the French of Christoforo Cattan (1558), itself derived from Italian and ultimately Arabic sources. Printed in London by John Wolfe, 1591. The earliest complete geomancy manual in the English language, covering the 16 figures, the twelve-house shield chart, the Judge and Witnesses, and the Wheel of Pythagoras.

The full PDF (153 MB microfilm scan) is hosted at [spiritwavelabs.com/downloads/cattan_geomancy_1591.pdf](https://spiritwavelabs.com/downloads/cattan_geomancy_1591.pdf). The OCR text layer has been extracted and is included here for AI processing and research use.

- [geomancy/cattan_geomancy_1591_text.txt](geomancy/cattan_geomancy_1591_text.txt) — Full OCR text extraction (481 KB, ~92,800 words, Elizabethan English)

### The Principles of Astrological Geomancy (Hartmann, 1889)
Franz Hartmann's systematic treatment of classical geomancy, published by the Theosophical Publishing Company, London. Draws directly on the Arabic tradition including al-Zanati. The most accessible English-language treatment of the classical system prior to the 20th-century occult revival. Public domain.

- [geomancy/hartmann_geomancy_1889.pdf](geomancy/hartmann_geomancy_1889.pdf)

---

### The I Ching (Legge, 1882)
James Legge's translation of the I Ching, published as Volume 16 of the *Sacred Books of the East* series. The standard scholarly English translation of the Chinese oracle. Public domain.

- [geomancy/legge_i_ching_1882.pdf](geomancy/legge_i_ching_1882.pdf)

## Geomancy — Islamic and Arabic Tradition

This section collects primary sources and key scholarly works on *ilm al-raml* (the science of the sand) — the Arabic geomancy tradition from which the Western system descends. The Arabic tradition predates the European one by several centuries and remained a living practice in the Islamic world long after geomancy declined in Europe.

### Penn Arabic Geomancy Manuscript, Ms. Codex 1918 (c. 1500)
Two Arabic geomancy texts from the University of Pennsylvania's Kislak Center for Special Collections. The second text is a commentary and abridgement of a longer work on *ilm al-raml* by Umar ibn Ahmad ibn Ali al-Khita'i. Arabic manuscript, c. 1500. Freely available via Archive.org (identifier: mscodex1918).

- [geomancy/penn_arabic_geomancy_manuscript_c1500.pdf](geomancy/penn_arabic_geomancy_manuscript_c1500.pdf)

---

### Kitab-i Surkhab dar 'Ilm-i Raml (Persian manuscript, 1528)
A Persian geomancy manuscript from Columbia University Libraries. Stated to be transmitted from sayings of the prophet Daniel. Contains numerous tables and inserts. Represents the living Persian geomancy tradition — the same cultural stream as Fal-e Hafez. Persian, 1528. Freely available via Archive.org (identifier: ldpd_13892500_000).

- [geomancy/surkhab_persian_geomancy_1528.pdf](geomancy/surkhab_persian_geomancy_1528.pdf)

## Fortune-Telling Manuscripts

### The Extract or Tables of Rotalo — Wellcome MS.957 (late 18th century)
A manuscript of lottery and fortune-telling tables, with five pen-drawn fortune-telling wheels, a note of "Numbers calculated by Count Cagliostro for the Lottery 1776 in London", and a 1794 watermark. The Wellcome Collection publishes it as seventy separate images and no assembled document, so this is those images compiled into a single PDF that can be read as a book. The images are the Wellcome's, released under the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark 1.0; the compilation is ours.

- [wellcome_ms957_compiled.pdf](wellcome_ms957_compiled.pdf) — 72 pages, 15.8 MB. Higher-resolution files on request.

## Manuscripts — Occult Authors

### Rimes et Raison — Éliphas Lévi (1871)

The poet's own notebook, in the hand of Alphonse Louis Constant (1810-1875), the former abbé who wrote as Éliphas Lévi. It holds unpublished verse, some of it dedicated to Auguste Barbier, to Mgr Dupanloup and to Victor Hugo, and its title page is signed, in his own hand, "par Eliphas Lévi": the manuscript carries the pen name rather than the man's name, which is the small fact worth having. Gérard Encausse, Papus, owned the notebook before the Sorbonne did.

It is held by the Bibliothèque interuniversitaire de la Sorbonne as MS 1799, 116 leaves, 180 x 140 mm, digitised by NuBIS. The library publishes it as 251 images and a 214 MB PDF, so this is those images compiled into one document that can be read as a book, with the library's own catalogue description, its credit line and the source link printed inside it. Nothing is cropped, nothing is re-ordered, and the leaves are otherwise unaltered.

Rights: the Sorbonne publishes the digitised manuscript under the **Licence Ouverte Etalab**, which permits reuse with attribution, and this compilation carries that credit. It is not a public domain work by date.

There is no transcription. The manuscript is written in a nineteenth-century hand, and a machine reading taken off a photograph would be a guess rather than a reading.

- [levi_rimes_et_raison_ms1799.pdf](levi_rimes_et_raison_ms1799.pdf): 253 pages, 44.7 MB. Higher-resolution files on request.
